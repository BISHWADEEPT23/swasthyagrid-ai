## Slide 2: The Problem: The Hidden Anatomy of Primary Care Stockouts
- **Slide Title**: **The Anatomy of a Preventable Crisis**
- **Subtitle**: *Why Primary Health Centres Run Out of Life-Saving Medicines*
- **Core Message**: Clinics stock out not from national shortages, but because inventory thresholds are static, visibility is fragmented, and warnings arrive too late.
- **Visual Layout**:
  - Three distinct failure cards with warning icons:
    1. **Static Threshold Trap**: Line chart showing surging consumption crossing flat reorder line with no warning.
    2. **Procurement Lead-Time Trap**: Calendar timeline showing 5–14 day supplier delivery delay.
    3. **Unsafe "Robin Hood" Transfers**: Flow diagram showing Clinic B transferring stock to Clinic A, causing Clinic B to collapse 48 hours later.
- **Key Bullet Points**:
  - Over 40% of rural primary clinics experience stockouts during seasonal disease outbreaks.
  - Traditional reorder rules assume static consumption, blinding doctors to surge acceleration.
  - Central emergency orders take 5–14 days to arrive—days after the clinic is already empty.
  - Blind, uncoordinated peer-to-peer transfers create secondary cascade failures.
- **Speaker Notes**:
  > *"When a rural clinic runs out of IV fluids during a monsoon outbreak, patients suffer. But why did they run out? Because legacy systems assume flat consumption and lack the intelligence to predict acceleration."*
