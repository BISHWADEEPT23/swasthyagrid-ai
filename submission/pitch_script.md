## [0:45 – 1:30] The Problem: Why Legacy Systems Fail

Traditional public health logistics relies on systems built thirty years ago. They fail because of three systemic traps:

First: **Static Min-Max Thresholds**. Legacy tools reorder when inventory drops below a fixed number. When a localized dengue or cholera outbreak hits, patient demand surges by thirty or forty percent. Static systems are blind to this acceleration, leaving doctors with less than twenty-four hours of warning.

Second: **The Procurement Lead-Time Trap**. By the time a rural clinic realizes it's in trouble, an emergency purchase order takes five to fourteen days to arrive from the central depot. The help arrives a week after the stockout has already occurred.

And third: **Unsafe "Robin Hood" Transfers**. When desperate health officers try to share supplies by phone, they guess. They take supplies from Clinic B to save Clinic A, only to plunge Clinic B into an unmanageable stockout two days later. Blind mutual aid simply spreads the crisis.
